Emergencies

Dental Emergency in Overland Park: What to Do in the First Hour

US Dr. Usha Sribollineni, DDS MDS, Prosthodontics July 20, 2026 5 min read

Dental emergencies are unusual in that what you do in the first hour genuinely changes the outcome, sometimes dramatically. A knocked-out adult tooth handled well in the first thirty minutes has a real chance of being saved. The same tooth handled poorly usually does not. Here is what to do, situation by situation.

The short version

  • A knocked-out adult tooth is the one true race against the clock. Handle it by the crown only, keep it moist, and get seen within the hour.
  • Spreading facial swelling, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or swelling that closes the eye is a medical emergency, not a dental one. Go to an emergency room.
  • Never put aspirin directly on a tooth or gum. It burns the tissue and does not help the pain.
  • Save the pieces of a broken tooth or a lost crown and bring them with you. They are often reusable.
  • Call your dental office first even outside hours. Most have an emergency instruction on the voicemail, and being triaged by a dentist saves time.

Go to an emergency room, not a dental office, if

Swelling is spreading across your face or down your neck, you are having difficulty swallowing or breathing, swelling has closed your eye, bleeding will not stop after twenty minutes of firm pressure, you may have broken your jaw, or you have had a significant blow to the head. These need hospital care first.

A knocked-out adult tooth

This is the only common dental emergency where minutes genuinely matter. The ligament cells on the root surface begin to die once the tooth is out and dry, and those cells are what allow it to reattach.

  1. Find the tooth and pick it up by the crown. The crown is the white part you normally see. Never touch or scrub the root.
  2. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk, or in your own saliva, or in saline. Use water only if nothing else is available, and only for a few seconds. Do not scrub it, do not use soap, do not dry it.
  3. If you can, put it back in the socket. Line it up the right way round, push it gently but firmly into place, and bite on a clean cloth to hold it there. A replanted tooth in its socket is in the best possible transport medium.
  4. If you cannot reimplant it, keep it moist. A cup of milk is the best readily available option. Failing that, hold it inside your cheek. Do not let it dry out and do not wrap it in tissue.
  5. Get to a dentist immediately. Call on the way. Within thirty minutes is excellent, within an hour is good, and beyond a couple of hours the outlook drops considerably.

Baby teeth are the exception

Do not attempt to reimplant a knocked-out baby tooth. Pushing it back can damage the developing adult tooth underneath. Keep the child comfortable, control any bleeding with gentle pressure, and arrange a dental appointment to check the area.

A broken or cracked tooth

Rinse gently with warm water, save any fragments you can find in milk or water, and apply a cold compress to the outside of the face for the swelling. If a sharp edge is cutting your tongue or cheek, orthodontic wax or a small piece of sugar-free gum over it is a reasonable temporary measure. Avoid chewing on that side entirely.

Depth is what determines urgency. A shallow enamel chip can wait. If yellow dentin is exposed, if it is sensitive to air or cold, or if you can see a pink or red spot in the centre where the nerve is exposed, it should be seen the same day. Fragments are genuinely useful, because a clean piece of a front tooth can sometimes be bonded straight back on with a better result than any filling material.

Severe toothache

Rinse with warm salt water, floss gently either side of the tooth in case food is trapped, and take whatever painkiller you would normally use according to the packet instructions. Keep your head elevated when you lie down, because the throbbing usually worsens flat. A cold compress outside the cheek helps.

Two things not to do: never place aspirin against the gum, which causes a chemical burn and does nothing for the pain, and avoid heat on the outside of the face, which can encourage an infection to spread.

If the pain is keeping you awake, or if it comes with any swelling, that is beyond the point where waiting is sensible.

Swelling or an abscess

A localised swelling on the gum next to a tooth needs prompt dental attention. Rinse with warm salt water several times a day and use a cold compress outside. Do not try to lance or squeeze it. If it drains on its own the pain often improves markedly, which feels like recovery but is not, because the source is still there.

What changes the level of urgency is the direction of spread. Swelling that is moving up towards the eye, back towards the ear, or down under the jaw and into the neck needs the same day, and if swallowing or breathing is affected, the emergency room.

A lost filling or crown

Uncomfortable rather than dangerous, but do not leave it long. Keep the crown safe and bring it with you, since a good proportion can simply be recemented. Temporary dental cement from a pharmacy is acceptable for a day or two if the crown seats fully without force. Avoid chewing on it, keep the area clean, and be gentle with cold, as exposed dentin is often very sensitive.

The reason not to wait is that the exposed tooth is unprotected, decays quickly, and can shift enough within a couple of weeks that the original crown no longer fits.

A broken orthodontic wire or bracket

If a wire is poking into your cheek, tuck it flat with the eraser end of a pencil and cover it with orthodontic wax. Do not cut it unless it is genuinely unavoidable. Contact your orthodontist for a repair appointment. Uncomfortable but not urgent.

Soft tissue injuries

Bitten lip, tongue, or cheek. Clean gently with water, apply firm pressure with clean gauze for fifteen to twenty minutes, and use a cold compress. If bleeding has not stopped after twenty minutes of continuous pressure, or if the cut is deep or gaping, go to an emergency room, as it may need stitches.

An emergency kit worth having

None of this is expensive, and having it in the house removes the scramble at the worst moment: clean gauze, a small container with a lid, temporary dental cement, orthodontic wax, an instant cold pack, whatever painkiller you normally take, and your dental office number saved in your phone rather than looked up in a panic.

Calling out of hours in Overland Park

Call your dental office first, even at night or at the weekend. Most practices, including Comfort Dental locations, have an outgoing message with instructions for urgent problems, and speaking to a dentist gets you triaged properly rather than sitting in an emergency room waiting area for something a dentist needs to treat anyway.

If you are a patient of mine at Comfort Dental in Overland Park and something has happened, call the office. If it is a true medical emergency involving your airway, swallowing, or spreading swelling, do not wait for a call back. Go to the nearest emergency department.

The one thing to remember

If an adult tooth has come out, keep it moist and get to a dentist within the hour. Everything else on this page can be managed for a few hours. That one cannot.
